Techne Hora · logo directions

Three ways to let “Hora” step back.

Each keeps IBM Plex Serif for “Techne” and the clock at ten o’clock. What changes is how much of the word “Hora” you can see. All files are real vectors with the letters converted to shapes, built in logo/_explorations/plex/.

Concept A

The clock is the word

Techne ◷

  1. Once upon a timeOnce upon a time, there was a craftsman named Techne who spent every evening chasing hours that slipped away.
  2. Every dayEvery day, owners told him the same thing: “I don’t have time.”
  3. One dayOne day, he stopped writing the word for hour and simply drew it.
  4. Because of thatBecause of that, the name became something you could read from across a parking lot.
  5. Because of thatBecause of that, the clock could stand alone on a van door, a shirt sleeve, or a phone screen, and people still knew it.
  6. Until finallyUntil finally, nobody needed to read “Hora” at all. They just saw their time come back.
Why it works
The boldest and simplest. The clock carries the whole second word, so the icon and the logo are the same idea.
Watch out for
Some people will only say “Techne.” Your full name still appears in text everywhere: the site title, cards, search results, invoices.
Concept B

The hour slipping away

Techne Hora, fading

  1. Once upon a timeOnce upon a time, there was an hour that every busy owner kept losing.
  2. Every dayEvery day, it faded a little more: a missed call, a late quote, a stack of invoices.
  3. One dayOne day, Techne showed up, held on to the craft, and let the busywork go.
  4. Because of thatBecause of that, the logo shows exactly that: Techne solid and certain, Hora fading letter by letter.
  5. Because of thatBecause of that, the empty space is the point. That’s the time you got back.
  6. Until finallyUntil finally, the hour wasn’t lost. It was freed.
Why it works
Tells the story in the type itself and keeps the full name readable. It feels the most editorial.
Watch out for
The fade needs good printing. Embroidery, vinyl and photocopies can only use one solid color, so that version loses the story. The icon is the weakest of the three.
Concept C

Signed on the dial

Techne + HORA on the clock face

  1. Once upon a timeOnce upon a time, the best watchmakers signed their name small on the face of every clock they built.
  2. Every dayEvery day, people glanced at those clocks without noticing the signature.
  3. One dayOne day, someone looked closer, found it, and trusted the maker more because of it.
  4. Because of thatBecause of that, Techne Hora puts “Techne” out front, big and plain, and signs “HORA” quietly on the dial.
  5. Because of thatBecause of that, from a distance it’s a confident name and a clock. Up close, it rewards you with the rest.
  6. Until finallyUntil finally, the hour is there for anyone who takes a closer look, the way you’d inspect a well-made tool.
Why it works
Keeps both words and the rewind arrow, and fits a craft brand, since watch dials really are signed this way. HORA fades out at small sizes on its own, which is the “lost in a good way” you described.
Watch out for
HORA is tiny by design, so below about 48px (and in embroidery) use the dial without the signature. It’s the busiest of the three marks.
Recommendation

Lead with C, keep A’s clean dial for small sizes.

“Signed on the dial” keeps the full name, the story and the arrow, and lets Hora disappear exactly when it should: at a distance and at small sizes. Below 48px, and for embroidery, swap in the plain dial from A. Concept B is the most poetic, but it can’t survive one-color printing, and vans and shirts are where your buyers will see you.
