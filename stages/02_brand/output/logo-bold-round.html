Techne Hora · bold round

Five marks with some depth.

Each one is drawn entirely from vector shapes: gradients, facets and shadows, with no photos and no bitmap effects. Every concept also has a flat version, because vans, shirts and invoices can’t print shading. Type appears only where a name belongs.

Concept 1

The Enamel Dial

A watchmaker’s dial with the name engraved on the bezel

  1. Once upon a timeThere was a word, techne, for work done with real skill.
  2. Every dayOwners looked at clocks and saw only how little time they had left.
  3. One dayA maker built them a clock worth looking at, and signed it on the bezel.
  4. Because of that“Techne” sits at the top like a crown, and “Hora” rests at the bottom like a hallmark.
  5. Because of thatThe logo feels like an heirloom tool, not an app.
  6. Until finallyPeople trust it before they’ve read a word.
Why it works
Premium and timeless. Both words live on the mark, so the icon alone says the full name.
Watch out for
The lettering on the bezel blurs below about 64px. Use a plain dial (no bezel text) for favicons.
Concept 2

The Rewind Ribbon

A chunky 3D arrow winds the clock backward

  1. Once upon a timeThere was a clock that only ever ran forward.
  2. Every dayIt took a little more of every owner’s evening.
  3. One daySomeone grabbed the hands and wound them back.
  4. Because of thatThe arrow became the hero, a thick ribbon of brick and coral.
  5. Because of thatIt pops off a van door from across the street.
  6. Until finallyThe brand meant one thing at a glance: time, given back.
Why it works
The loudest and most memorable from a distance. Great on signs, vans and social.
Watch out for
Close to a generic “refresh” or “sync” icon. It needs the name nearby to be ownable.
Concept 3

The T-Hand

The minute hand is a capital T: Techne literally sets the hour

  1. Once upon a timeThere was a clock with an ordinary minute hand.
  2. Every dayIt pointed at twelve and meant nothing in particular.
  3. One dayThe hand grew a crossbar and became a T, for Techne.
  4. Because of thatThe craft is what moves the time.
  5. Because of thatThe app icon reads as both a clock and a letter, even at 24px.
  6. Until finallyOne small shape carried the whole name: Techne, setting the Hora.
Why it works
The strongest system mark: one idea, ownable, readable tiny, and it works flat. The tile frame makes it a ready app or social icon.
Watch out for
The rounded-square tile can look like a phone app. For signage, drop the tile and use the dial alone.
Concept 4

The Sundial

A T-shaped gnomon whose shadow tells the hour

  1. Once upon a timeBefore clocks, there were sundials, and “hora” was measured by a shadow.
  2. Every dayThe sun moved, and a shadow quietly marked the time.
  3. One dayThe gnomon took the shape of a T.
  4. Because of thatTechne casts the shadow, and the shadow is the hour hand.
  5. Because of thatThe logo tells the oldest story about time in one image.
  6. Until finallyThe hour became something the craft makes, not something you lose.
Why it works
The most original and the best story. It would be stunning in the hero, on the About page, or as a 3D animation.
Watch out for
It’s an illustration, not a logo. It loses its detail below about 48px and can’t be embroidered. Use it as a brand illustration beside a simpler mark.
Concept 5

The Hourglass H

The H of Hora is an hourglass; the crossbar is its brass collar

  1. Once upon a timeThere was a letter H, two posts and a bar.
  2. Every dayOwners watched their sand run out.
  3. One dayThe space between the posts filled with glass.
  4. Because of thatThe H became an hourglass, with most of the sand already safe at the bottom.
  5. Because of thatThe H can stand in for itself inside the name: Techne Hora.
  6. Until finallyThe hours weren’t running out. They were piling up.
Why it works
Clever and very simple to print. The H drops straight into the wordmark.
Watch out for
Hourglasses usually mean “time is running out”, the opposite of the message. It moves away from the clock you already have.
Recommendation

Make the T-Hand the logo, and the Sundial its story.

The T-Hand is the one idea that works everywhere. The minute hand is a T, so the craft literally sets the hour. It stays readable at 20px, works flat on a van, and doesn’t need the arrow to make sense.

The Sundial is the most beautiful thing here, but it’s an illustration. Put it in the homepage hero and on the About page, where its story (a T casting the hour) can breathe. The Enamel Dial is the premium alternative if you want both words on the mark itself.
